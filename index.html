Q1 · MCQ
Which CMMI maturity level primarily focuses on project planning, requirements management, and configuration management?
Core concept — likely on exam
1 mark ▼
Answer: Level 2 — Managed
From the slides: Level 2 introduces Requirements Management (REQM) — know what the customer asked for, Project Planning (PP) — estimate tasks, people, cost, and schedule, Project Monitoring & Control (PMC) — compare actual progress with the plan, and Configuration Management (CM) — control versions and changes.
Slide note: "Result: each project can be managed, even if the whole organization does not yet use one standard process."

Expected Cost & CMMI

Chapter 8 · Section 2

9 marks
Q2
Create a comprehensive table of the 5 CMMI Maturity Levels — definition, key steps, and advantages.
Long answer · memorize all 5 levels
4 marks ▼
LevelNameDefinition (from slides)Key Steps / ActionsAdvantage
1 Initial Work is ad hoc and chaotic. Success depends on individual effort rather than a reliable process. No written requirements, no plan. Testing happens late or only when bugs appear. Schedules depend on "who is available today." Starting point — awareness that a process is needed.
2 Managed The team plans, tracks, manages requirements, and controls changes at the project level. REQM: know what the customer asked for. PP: estimate tasks/cost/schedule. PMC: compare actual vs plan. CM: control versions and changes. Each project can be managed and tracked, even without an org-wide standard process.
3 Defined Processes are documented, trained, and applied across the whole organization — not just per project. Requirements Development, Technical Solution, Verification, Validation, Risk Management, Organizational Training — same for every team. Consistency across all projects — easier to scale and onboard new teams.
4 Quantitatively Managed The organization controls performance using measures and statistical thinking — results are predictable. Track: defects per module, effort per feature, test pass rate, schedule variance and cycle time. Data-driven decisions — cost and quality can be predicted accurately before release.
5 Optimizing The organization uses evidence to prevent problems and innovate, not just react. Find root cause → improve process (checklist/tool/training) → deploy innovation across teams → measure if quality/cost/speed improved → repeat. Highest quality and efficiency — improvement becomes part of daily work.
From slides (exam wording): "Best maturity result: Level 5. Best practical first goal: Level 3 — Defined." If asked where a low-maturity org should start → Level 2, then Level 3.

Risk Management

Chapter 7 · Section 3

15 marks
Q4
Detail the 5 main steps in risk management activities. Explanation + QuickBite example for each.
From slides: Identifying → Determining → Evaluation → Planning → Documenting/Communicating
5 marks ▼
1
Identifying Risk
Find all possible threats before they happen. "You cannot manage what you have not identified."
QB: App crashes during peak dinner hours (7–9 PM), running out of marketing budget before launch, competitor launching a 50% discount on launch day.
2
Determining Probabilities (Analysis & Assessment)
Measure each risk: Risk Score = Probability × Severity. Goal: measuring the "size" of the threat to prioritize resources.
QB: App crash — Probability: High, Severity: High → Score 9/10 (Critical). Late deliveries — Probability: Medium, Severity: Medium → Score 5/10 (Moderate).
3
Evaluation & Prioritization
Rank risks using a Risk Heat Map. "Focus your resources where the impact is greatest."
QB: Red Zone — app stability and payment security (must fix before launch). Yellow Zone — marketing costs and delivery speed. Green Zone — office supply shortages (accept and ignore).
4
Planning Responses (Treatment & Mitigation — The 4 T's)
Choose a strategy for each risk: Terminate, Treat, Transfer, or Tolerate.
QB: Treat the app crash risk — hire extra servers and run stress tests to prevent crashes.
5
Documenting & Communicating (Monitoring & Review)
"Risk management is a living document, not a one-time task." The market changes daily — continuously check for new and evolving threats.
QB: After 1 month, discovered "Driver Shortage" risk. Adjusted immediately — increased driver bonuses to ensure supply and maintain delivery speed.
Q5
Identify the 4 primary risk response strategies (Terminate, Treat, Transfer, Tolerate). Explain the specific purpose of each.
The 4 T's — directly from slide 7
3 marks ▼
🚫Terminate (Avoidance)
Stopping the activity entirely to eliminate the risk. Used when the risk is too high to manage.
QB: Deciding not to deliver in high-crime areas to protect driver safety.
🔧Treat (Reduction)
Taking action to lower the probability or impact of the risk to an acceptable level.
QB: Hiring extra servers and running stress tests to prevent app crashes.
↗Transfer (Sharing)
Shifting the financial or operational burden of the risk to a third party.
QB: Purchasing insurance for delivery riders to shift the liability.
✓Tolerate (Acceptance)
Acknowledging the risk and bearing the cost if it occurs. Usually for low-impact risks.
QB: Accepting that 1% of orders might be slightly late due to traffic.
Q6
Differentiate between the types of project risks — Operational, Financial, Compliance, Reputational, and Cyber. One QuickBite example each.
From slides 9 & 14
3 marks ▼
Operational
Failed delivery logistics, GPS errors, or restaurant partner delays.
QB: A GPS glitch sends drivers to wrong locations, causing massive delays.
Financial
Budget overruns and lower-than-expected sales.
QB: Running out of marketing budget before the launch date, preventing user acquisition.
Compliance
New labor laws regarding gig-economy workers or food safety regulations.
QB: A new law requires all delivery riders to be classified as full-time employees.
Reputational
Negative publicity from cold food, rude drivers, or social media scandals.
QB: A viral video of a driver eating a customer's fries ruins the brand's trust.
Cyber
Threats from data breaches, hacking, or IT system failures.
QB: Hackers steal 10,000 customer credit card records from the app's database.
Q7
Explain how to calculate Expected Monetary Value (EMV). Formula + table with Risk, Probability, Impact, and EMV columns.
From slides 10–13 — QuickBite EMV example
4 marks ▼
EMV = Probability × Impact  |  Total Project EMV = Σ all EMVs
Negative impact = risk/threat (loss). Positive impact = opportunity (gain). The total tells you the risk reserve needed.
RiskProbabilityImpact ($)EMV ($)
App crash 30% (0.30) −200,000 −60,000
Late deliveries 40% (0.40) −50,000 −20,000
Marketing success 25% (0.25) +120,000 +30,000
Total Project EMV −$50,000
Interpretation: QuickBite needs a risk reserve of at least $50,000. The negative total means threats currently outweigh identified opportunities.
