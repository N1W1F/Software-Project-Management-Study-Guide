Q1 · MCQ
Which CMMI maturity level primarily focuses on project planning, requirements management, and configuration management?
Core concept — likely on exam
1 mark ▼
Answer: Level 2 — Managed
Level 2 is the only level that specifically introduces: Requirements Management (REQM), Project Planning (PP), Project Monitoring & Control (PMC), and Configuration Management (CM). Each project is planned and tracked individually at this level.
Tip: Level 2 = project-level control. Level 3 = org-wide standards. They are commonly confused on exams.

Expected Cost & CMMI

Chapter 8 · Section 2

9 marks
Q2
Create a comprehensive table of the 5 CMMI Maturity Levels — definition, key steps, and advantages.
Long answer — memorize all 5 rows
4 marks ▼
Level Name Definition Key Steps / Actions Advantage
1 Initial Chaotic, hero-dependent. Success relies on talented individuals, not a repeatable process. No formal planning or docs. Ad-hoc testing. Starting point — awareness of the problem is the first step.
2 Managed Each project is planned and tracked individually with requirements and change control. Requirements Mgmt, Project Planning, Monitoring & Control, Config Management. Projects become controllable — track progress and changes per project.
3 Defined One standard process documented and applied across the entire organization. Standard SDLC, Risk Management, Verification, Validation, unified team training. Consistency across all projects — easier to scale, onboard, and grow.
4 Quantitatively Managed Performance measured statistically — results are predictable using real data. Track defects/module, effort/feature, test pass rate, schedule variance. Data-driven decisions — cost and quality can be predicted accurately.
5 Optimizing Continuous improvement institutionalized via root cause analysis and innovation. Root Cause Analysis → fix process → deploy improvement → measure effect → repeat. Highest quality & efficiency — organization learns and improves automatically each cycle.
Exam tip: Best possible result = Level 5. Best practical first goal for a low-maturity org = Level 3.

Risk Management

Chapter 7 · Section 3

15 marks
Q4
Detail the 5 main steps in risk management — explanation + QuickBite example for each.
5 steps · one example each · QuickBite case study
5 marks ▼
1
Risk Identification
Find all possible threats before they occur. Use brainstorming, checklists, and historical data.
QB: Identified 3 key risks — app crash at peak hours, marketing budget running out before launch, and a competitor launching a 50% discount on day 1.
2
Risk Analysis & Assessment
Measure each risk: Score = Probability × Severity. Determines how serious the risk is.
QB: App crash at peak = Probability High × Severity High = Score 9/10 (Critical). Late deliveries = Score 5/10 (Moderate).
3
Risk Evaluation & Prioritization
Rank risks using a Risk Heat Map. Focus resources on the highest-impact risks first.
QB: Red Zone (fix before launch) = app stability & payment security. Yellow Zone = marketing costs. Green Zone = minor office supply shortages (acceptable).
4
Risk Response / Treatment
Choose a strategy for each risk: Terminate, Treat, Transfer, or Tolerate (the 4 T's).
QB: Treat the app crash risk → hire extra servers and run stress tests before launch day.
5
Monitoring, Documentation & Communication
Continuously track risks, update the risk register, and communicate status — the environment changes constantly.
QB: One month after launch, discovered a new "driver shortage" risk → immediately raised driver bonuses to maintain supply.
Q5
Identify the 4 primary risk response strategies (Terminate, Treat, Transfer, Tolerate) and explain each.
The 4 T's — purpose + QuickBite example
3 marks ▼
🚫
Terminate (Avoid)
Eliminate the activity causing the risk entirely. Used when the risk is too high to manage by any other means.
QB: Stop delivering to high-crime areas completely to protect driver safety.
🔧
Treat (Reduce)
Take action to lower the probability of the risk occurring or reduce its impact if it does occur.
QB: Add extra servers and run stress tests to prevent app crashes during peak hours.
↗
Transfer (Share)
Shift the financial or operational burden of the risk to a third party — does not eliminate the risk, just moves who bears it.
QB: Buy insurance for delivery riders — financial liability for accidents moves to the insurer.
✓
Tolerate (Accept)
Accept the risk and absorb the cost if it occurs. Used for low-impact or low-probability risks where treatment costs more than the risk itself.
QB: Accept that 1% of orders may arrive slightly late due to traffic — not worth extra investment to fix.
Q6
Differentiate between the 5 types of project risks — Operational, Financial, Compliance, Reputational, Cyber.
One QuickBite example per type
3 marks ▼
Operational
Failure in internal systems, processes, people, or tools.
QB: A GPS glitch sends drivers to wrong addresses, causing massive delays across all orders.
Financial
Budget overruns or revenue falling short of projections.
QB: Marketing budget runs out before launch, making it impossible to acquire enough users to break even.
Compliance
Legal, regulatory, or industry violations that expose the company to penalties.
QB: A new law requires all gig workers to be classified as full-time employees — restructuring costs are enormous.
Reputational
Damage to brand image and customer trust, often from public incidents.
QB: A viral video of a driver eating a customer's order spreads widely, destroying QuickBite's brand image.
Cyber
Data breaches, hacking attacks, or critical IT system failures.
QB: Hackers breach the app's database and steal 10,000 customers' credit card records.
Q7
Explain how to calculate Expected Monetary Value (EMV) — formula + structured table.
Columns: Risk · Probability · Impact · EMV
4 marks ▼
EMV = Probability × Impact  |  Total EMV = Σ all EMVs
Negative impact = threat/loss. Positive impact = opportunity/gain. The total EMV tells you how much risk reserve the project needs.
Risk Probability Impact ($) EMV ($)
App crash during peak hours 30% (0.30) −200,000 −60,000
Late deliveries 40% (0.40) −50,000 −20,000
Marketing campaign success 25% (0.25) +120,000 +30,000
Total Project EMV −$50,000
Interpretation: QuickBite needs a risk reserve of at least $50,000 to cover expected losses from identified risks. The negative total indicates threats outweigh identified opportunities.
